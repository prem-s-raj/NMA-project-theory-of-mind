Theory of Mind (ToM) has been defined as an ability to attribute independent mental states to self and others in order to explain and predict behaviour (Castelli et al., 2000). Notably, ToM dynamics can be studied by observing how subjects attribute intentions and mental states to simple moving objects when their motion resembles goal-oriented social interaction. In autism, this capacity is greatly reduced, resulting in social and affective deficits (Baron-Cohen et al., 1985). 

Here, we aim to identify which brain regions are active in the social cognition task and we hypothesize, based on meta-analyses of mentalizing behaviour that temporoparietal junction (TPJ), medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), inferior and superior temporal sulcus (STS) will be activated during the mental-interaction condition. 

To test this, we utilized the Human Connectome Project (HCP) fMRI dataset, where participants were presented with a modified Frithe-Happe animation  consisting  of geometrical shapes either interacting in a meaningful way, or moving randomly (control condition).

We modeled trial-level brain activity across 360 regions using a generalized linear model with hemodynamic response function, isolating per-region activation estimates for 100 subjects performing the aforementioned task. Having identified the mentalization regions, we hypothesized that information relevant to stimulus type is reflected in their joint dynamics. To test this, we created  different multivariate decoding models, using the activation patterns described above as inputs. 

Preliminary random forest analyses achieved 85% subject-generalizable accuracy (F1 = 0.85 for both classes) in distinguishing perceived mental interaction from random motion, with the most informative ROIs corresponding to regions consistently implicated in Theory of Mind and social cognition. 

These data suggest that the joint, multivariate activity of mentalization regions in tandem encode information relevant to mentalization. Further studies should seek to extend these findings using voxel-wise methodologies.
